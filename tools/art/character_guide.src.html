Lodestone characters

How your character sheet becomes the template for every person, monster and boss: the exact frame the game expects, Krita templates that sit on the battle-map grid, and a step-by-step Krita AI workflow for turnarounds, paper-doll parts and animation. Every rule here is a proposal until you mark it yours.

  • 2:1 dimetric, same as maps
  • frame 192 × 192 per cell
  • feet at (96, 160)
  • head at 3 levels = 96 px
  • draw 5 views, mirror 3
  • ink #373335
  • model sheet 768 / view

Your sheet, as a template

What the template keeps from it: the chibi build (about 2.3 heads tall), the five turnaround views, one diamond under every figure, the idle / walk / attack / cast / hurt set, parts drawn on their own, and the eight swatches, which become the colour keys the game recolours.

What the template pins down, so every character lands on the board the same way:

  • The diamond is exact. 128 × 64 per cell at the same 2:1 angle as the battle maps, and the feet sit on its centre. On the sheet the diamonds are hand-drawn and a little different in each view.
  • The views are exact. S, SE, E, NE and N, rendered from the board's own camera. Your top row is close to SE, SE-ish, E, N and NE; the template names them so the game knows which is which.
  • The size is fixed. Every person's head reaches 3 levels (96 px at art size), so a party reads as a set and a 2-level cliff hides two thirds of a piece behind it.
  • Light comes from above, not from one side, so a view can be mirrored and still look lit by the maps' upper-left sun.
  • Most motion is cut-out. Walking and idling in eight directions come from moving your parts. Drawn frames are only for the big combat beats.

The rule behind the table: an n × n piece has a 192n square frame with its feet at (96n, 160n). The diamond fills the bottom of the frame and the rest is headroom for hats, raised swords and spells. The engine draws everything at half size on a 720p screen, exactly like the tiles.

Never paint ground, a base or a shadow into a frame. The engine adds the contact shadow, the team ring and the board-game base.

Eight directions, five drawings

Facings are named by where the piece looks on screen. On this grid a step from cell to cell runs along the diamond's edges, so in a fight a piece only ever faces SE, SW, NE or NW. Free roaming adds the straight S, E, N, W.

  • Draw S, SE, E, NE, N. The game mirrors SE into SW, E into W and NE into NW.
  • Do SE and NE first: with their mirrors they cover every fight.
  • If a character is lopsided (sling on one shoulder, an eyepatch), draw its SW, W and NW too; the game prefers a drawn view over a mirror.
  • Turning the camera (Q) just changes which view shows. Nothing extra to draw.

Krita setup (once, on the MacBook)

You already have Krita AI Diffusion running with ZavyChroma XL for the battle maps. Characters need three more things.

  1. Control models. Docker gear › Connection › the managed server's package list. Make sure the SDXL control packages are installed: Line Art, Depth, Pose and the Reference (IP-Adapter) package. Restart the server if you add any.
  2. A style called "Lodestone piece". Docker gear › Styles › duplicate "Lodestone battle", rename it, keep ZavyChroma XL, and paste the style prompt and negative below. Leave sampler and steps at their defaults. Turn on Fixed seed while you work on one character so changes come from your edits, not the dice.
  3. The palette. Palette docker › Import resource › art/templates/characters/lodestone-characters.gpl. It shows up as "Lodestone characters".
  4. A grid for clean slot copies. Grid and Guides docker: Rectangle, spacing 768 × 768 for model sheets and parts, 192 × 192 for hero sprite sheets (384 large, 576 boss). Turn on View › Snap To › Grid when you select a slot.
  5. Python for the exporter (Terminal, once): python3 -m pip install --user pillow numpy psd-tools. Or skip it: push the .ora and ask Claude to export.

A new character, step by step

Example: the hero from your sheet. You decide the look and the id; the steps are the same for every person on the roster. Save working files in art/characters/<id>/.

1. Rough the key view (SE)

  1. Open hero/hero-model-sheet.psd, save it as art/characters/hero/hero-model.kra.
  2. On paint 1 generated, rough the costume over the SE mannequin with flat palette colours: cloak shape, tunic, boots, hair, the sword on the back. Ugly is fine. Shapes and colours are what the model follows; the mannequin only fixes size, angle and where the feet go.
  3. Select the SE slot (snap to the 768 grid) so generation stays inside it.
  4. Add control layers: Depth from views/se-depth.png at 50–60%, and Line Art from views/se-lineart.png at 30–45% (low, because the mannequin has no costume). Import the PNGs as hidden layers first and point the control layers at them.
  5. Prompt with the view words plus your costume words, and Refine at 55–70%. Over bare mannequin blocking with no rough, go 75–90%.
  6. Generate a batch, keep the best, paint over what's wrong. When you're happy, copy it into the KEY slot.
Key view (SE) · replace the bracketed words with your design
three-quarter front view, turned to the right, [green hooded cloak with round clasp, blue tunic, leather belt with pouches, brown boots, messy brown hair, sword on the back, rolled bedroll]

2. Turn it around (S, E, NE, N)

  1. Put a copy of the KEY view on its own layer named REF key (hidden). Add a Reference control layer pointing at it, 60–80%. This is what keeps the same face, cloak and colours in every view.
  2. For each empty slot: rough the costume over that slot's mannequin (copying shapes from the key view helps a lot), select the slot, set Depth to that view's views/<dir>-depth.png at 50–60%, swap the view words, Refine at 50–65%. Same seed.
  3. Do NE and N next to each other so the back of the cloak and the pack match.
  4. Check details that must stay on one side (the clasp, the sword grip over a shoulder) against the key view. View › Mirror View (M) shows what the mirrored SW, W and NW will look like.
View words · swap into the prompt per slot
S:  front view, facing the viewer, symmetrical pose
SE: three-quarter front view, turned to the right
E:  side view, profile facing right
NE: three-quarter back view, turned away to the upper right, face hidden
N:  back view, facing away from the viewer, face hidden
Faster option once a design is settled: select all five slots and Refine the whole sheet at once with this prompt, the whole-sheet control-depth.png and the Reference layer. One pass sees every view together, which helps consistency, then fix single slots as above.
Whole-sheet turnaround
character turnaround sheet, the same character shown five times in a grid, front view, three-quarter front, side profile, three-quarter back, back view, identical costume and colours in every view, [costume words]

3. Clean up for the game

  1. Values: flatten each material to light / mid / dark. Light on top, dark under the chin, under the hem and down the far side. No side light, no rim light.
  2. Outline: merge a copy of the figure, then Layer › Layer Style › Stroke: 12 px, outside, #373335. That is exactly 3 px after the 4× scale-down. Inner lines by hand at about 8 px in the same ink.
  3. Palette lock: Filter › Map › Palettize with "Lodestone characters", no dithering. Or let the exporter do it with --lock.
  4. Read test: zoom to 25% (art size) and 12.5% (the game at 720p). If the face or the silhouette feature disappears, push it bigger.
  5. Export a copy: File › Export › hero-model.ora. Then python3 tools/art/char_art.py export art/characters/hero/hero-model.ora --id hero --lock writes hero_stand_s|se|e|ne|n.png.

4. Cut the paper doll

  1. Open hero-parts-front.psd (and later -back), save as art/characters/hero/hero-parts-front.kra.
  2. Copy each finished view from the model sheet (snap-select the slot) and paste it into the same slot of the parts file. The feet must land on the pink dot.
  3. Split it onto the part layers: Similar Colour Selection or Freehand Selection, then Cut and Paste into the part layer. Parts keep their place; never move them.
  4. Paint what hides under each moving part: torso under the near arm, hips under the tunic, cloak lining behind the arm. The pink circles are the pivots the game rotates around, so draw a little past each joint.
  5. Variations for random recruits: duplicate a part layer and rename it part 08 hair or hat 2. Each variant exports as its own file.
  6. Export a copy and run char_art.py export … --id hero --lock. Out come parts/se/torso.png and friends.

Animation

Two kinds, on purpose. Cut-out covers walking and idling in all eight directions for free; you draw nothing more. Key frames cover what cut-out can't sell: attack, cast, hurt and down, only in SE and NE.

AnimFramesfpsHow
idle46cut-out (or 4 drawn frames for a showy character)
walk610cut-out; draw only if the gait needs it
attack612drawn; damage lands on frame 3
cast610drawn; the spell releases on frame 4, glow on the fx layer
hurt28drawn (flinch, recoil)
down26drawn; holds the last frame

Key poses with Krita AI

  1. Open hero-anim-canvas.psd (one 768 view, same anchor). Rough the pose on paint 1 pose: a stick figure or a quick gesture over the SE mannequin.
  2. Add a Pose control layer and build the skeleton from your rough (the button next to the layer), then drag joints until the pose reads. 60–80%.
  3. Add the Reference layer with the KEY view at 60–75%. Prompt with the view words and the action, Refine at 60–75%.
  4. Clean up exactly as in step 3. Keep the feet on the pink dot unless the pose lunges, and even then the back foot stays on it.
Action words · add to the SE or NE prompt
attack wind-up: winding up a sword swing over the shoulder, weight on the back foot
attack hit: mid sword swing, blade extended forward, cloak flaring behind
cast: one arm raised, finger pointing up, small glowing swirl above the hand
hurt: flinching backwards, eyes shut, arms pulled in
down: sitting on the ground, slumped, dazed

In-betweens on the timeline

  1. Window › Workspace › Animation. Set the fps from the table. Make paint 1 pose an animated layer and place your key poses on their frames (attack: wind-up on 1–2, hit on 3).
  2. Turn on Onion Skins and draw the frames between. Keep them simple; at 48 px tall two strong poses and a smear read better than smooth motion.
  3. Hide the GUIDE layers. File › Render Animation › Image Sequence, folder art/characters/hero/renders/, base name hero_attack_se_.
  4. python3 tools/art/char_art.py pack art/characters/hero/renders --id hero --class hero --lock turns it into hero_attack_se.png at game size.
Small fixes are often quicker straight in hero-sheet.psd at shipping size: every frame has its diamond, anchor and label, and the export picks up only the frames you painted. The Krita AI plugin's Animation workspace can refine every keyframe with one setting, which is worth a test once a character's style is set.

Monsters and bosses

Monsters (small and large)

  • Pick the class by footprint, not by look: 1 × 1 is small (or hero for anything person-sized), 2 × 2 is large.
  • The mannequin is a dummy quadruped. Ignore its anatomy, keep its footprint, height band and facing. Beasts that walk on two legs can use the hero templates.
  • Facing must read in SE and NE alone: eyes, mouth, horns or weapon point along the facing.
  • Monsters may bring their own ramps (export with --palette or without --lock), but leave saturated red and blue to the team rings.
  • Earth to Alien: past the Wall, anatomy can get as strange as you like. The camera, ink, light and frame never change, so strange creatures still stand on the same board.
  • Quadruped parts: tail, legs far, body, legs near, head, ears or horns. Large walks are worth drawing when the gait is the creature's personality.

Bosses (3 × 3)

  • Build parts first: head, arms, core, and a weak point if the fight has one. A phase change can then swap one part (part 05 head phase 2) instead of a redraw.
  • One clear telegraph pose per attack (the wind-up frames). The player has to read it from across the board.
  • Frame 576, feet at (288, 480), head between 6 and 10 levels. The model sheet works at 1.33× for bosses, so paint big shapes and few details.
  • Bosses don't roam: the template has no S, E, N walks, and adds move and special rows.
  • Tall bosses hide terrain behind them. Keep the silhouette broad at the bottom and narrow at the top so tiles behind stay readable.

The rules, short

  • C1Same camera as the maps: 2:1 dimetric, orthographic, tile 128 × 64, level 32 px.
  • C2Frame 192n square for an n × n footprint, feet at (96n, 160n).
  • C3Heads: small ~1.5 levels, people 3, large ~4, bosses 6 to 10.
  • C4Bodies inside the footprint; heads, tails and weapons may overhang half a cell.
  • C5No ground, base or shadow in the frame.
  • C6Eight facings by screen direction; combat uses the diagonals.
  • C7Draw S, SE, E, NE, N; SW, W, NW mirror unless lopsided.
  • C8Light from above and in front, never one side, so mirrors stay true.
  • C9SE and NE first; S, E, N for roaming.
  • C10Ink #373335: 3 px outside, 2 px inside at game size (12 / 8 on the model sheet).
  • C11Flat fills, one light and one dark per material, no texture.
  • C12Only the key ramp colours, so the game can recolour.
  • C13Pieces are the warmest, brightest thing on the map; red and blue belong to team rings.
  • C14Big head, two dot eyes, one strong silhouette feature.
  • C15Idle and walk are cut-out from parts.
  • C16Drawn frames for attack, cast, hurt, down in SE and NE.
  • C17Hit on attack frame 3, release on cast frame 4.
  • C18Feet stay on the anchor; the engine moves pieces between cells.
  • C19Glows, dust and trails on the fx layer.
  • C20Parts drawn in place; assembly is stacking.
  • C21Front stack for S, SE, E; back stack for NE, N.
  • C22Paint what hides under every moving part.
  • C23Variants are extra part layers with a number.
  • C24Monsters: same camera and rules; quad or biped part set.
  • C25A monster's facing reads in SE and NE alone.
  • C26Bosses are parts first; they don't roam.
  • C27Working files in art/characters/<id>/.
  • C28Game files in game/art/source/characters/<id>/, named <id>_<anim>_<dir>.png.
  • C29Ids are your placeholders, lower_snake.

What the game still needs

None of this is built yet. In order of usefulness:

  1. Sprite node. Reads hero.json, builds the animations from the strips, puts the anchor on the cell, draws at half size. Falls back to today's pawn when art is missing, so characters can arrive one at a time.
  2. Facing. Pieces remember which way they last stepped and turn toward whoever they act on. The camera turn (Q) is applied, then a drawn view or a mirror is picked.
  3. Animation states. idle, walk, attack, cast, hurt, down hooked into the existing move hops and attack lunges. Damage numbers wait for the hit frame.
  4. Paper doll and recolour. Stacks parts in the right order per facing, swings them around the pivots for idle and walk, and swaps the key ramps per character so one set of parts covers the roster and random recruits.
  5. Big footprints. 2 × 2 and 3 × 3 units in the combat grid: occupancy, paths, targeting any covered cell, pushes, draw order.
  6. Free roaming. A walk-around mode on the same iso board: WASD or click-to-move, eight facings, step up one level at most, camera follow, party followers, event cards on interaction.
  7. Polish. Contact shadow and team base under sprites, HP pips at the head band, ghost outline from the sprite's silhouette, a test that every character has its SE and NE.

When it goes wrong

ProblemFix
The camera drifts to a low or side angle.Raise Depth to 65–70%; keep "high orthographic camera looking down at 30 degrees" in the style prompt.
Views don't look like the same character.Raise Reference to 80%, rough each view's colours by copying from the key view, keep the seed fixed, and try the whole-sheet pass.
The model adds a floor, grass or shadow.It's in the negative already; select only the figure's area when refining, then erase anything below the feet.
Lines go thin or grey after the scale-down.Outline at 12 px on the model sheet (Stroke layer style), not thinner; lock the palette after the stroke.
A mirrored view looks wrong.The character is lopsided: draw its own SW, W, NW (row 1 of the sprite sheet has slots for them).
A part rotates and leaves a hole.Paint further under the joint on the part beneath (C22).
Exported frames are empty.The paint is on a layer whose name doesn't start with paint or part NN, or the layer is hidden.
Krita shows a layer mask on every layer of an older battle-map PSD.Those files were saved before a fix on 2026-10-27; pull the repo for the regenerated templates (or use the .ora).
