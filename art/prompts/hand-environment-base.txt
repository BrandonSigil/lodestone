CAMERA (essential): 2:1 dimetric, the pixel-art style of isometric. A square tile grid turned 45 degrees and looked at from about 30 degrees above, so every floor tile is a diamond twice as wide as it is tall (every ground edge runs 2 across to 1 down). Orthographic: no perspective, no vanishing point, parallel edges stay parallel across the whole picture, no lens blur, no tilt-shift, no fisheye. The map is a grid of equal square cells; nothing walkable is smaller than a cell. Uprights are perfectly vertical.

HEIGHT: the ground is built from stacked blocks in terraces and levels, each step up one clear block high, so you can count the steps. Only two side faces ever show: the left faces are mid-tone and the right faces are the darkest; the tops are the lightest and calmest. Never draw the backs or undersides. Cliffs and drops show their full face with the material's courses or grain, and the map ends in an edge where the ground drops away into the cream paper: no horizon, no sky, no skybox. A few small loose pieces may fall away from the edge. Stairs are plain flights of low wide steps, one level a step, all the same size.

WALLS AND FLOORS: walls up front are short and thick so they never hide the floor behind them; walls at the back are tall. Floors and tops are big, calm and readable; detail lives on the cliff faces, walls and a few big props. Props stay few and big.

LIGHT: one soft sun from the upper left, flat and even, tops brightest, no hard cast shadows, nothing glowing except at most one small warm accent.

STYLE: flat soft watercolour and ink, like a children's picture book. Soft flat painted fills with a little soft mottle and a faint paper grain over everything, one lighter and one darker tone per surface, no gradients except a faint tone on stone or ground. A soft, slightly wobbly dark warm charcoal outline (#373335, never pure black) round each block, prop and edge, like a felt marker. Chunky simple readable shapes, gentle detail.

PALETTE: gentle, muted and warm, with cream paper (#eae8d6) around the map. The darkest tone is a deep tint of the level's own colours, never black. Choose the rest to fit the place above, a handful of colours for the whole picture.

Avoid: perspective or a vanishing point, a fisheye or wide-angle look, a skybox or horizon, photorealism, 3d render, glossy materials, neon, strong cast shadows, true isometric (30 degree grid lines), pixel art, people and monsters, text, letters, numbers, UI, borders, a title.
