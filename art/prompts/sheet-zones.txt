LODESTONE TERRAIN ZONE SHEET (exploration, many zones on one page)

Make a reference sheet in the same layout as the attached sheet: a short title at the top, then a grid of zone panels, 4 panels across and 2 down. Each panel has:
1. A label plate with the zone name.
2. One small floating island diorama of that zone, seen in a 2:1 dimetric game view (tile diamonds exactly twice as wide as tall, orthographic, no perspective). The island is about 5 by 5 tiles, built from chunky square blocks stacked in terraces of 1 to 3 steps, with cliff faces showing stacked stone courses, one course per step. Show one path or stair that connects the low and high ground, and 2 to 4 big props.
3. Under it, a 5 x 5 grid of square swatches, same order in every panel:
   row 1: main ground, ground variant, path or road, hard floor, rough rock (blank tops seen straight down, no objects on them)
   row 2: water or liquid, hazard ground, second floor variant, overgrown ground, special ground (blank tops seen straight down)
   row 3: cliff side, built wall side, waterfall or liquid side, cliff side variant, hazard side (seen straight on, 4 equal stone courses)
   row 4: small props alone on cream: bush, boulder, crate or barrel, light source, small detail
   row 5: tall props alone on cream: tree, second tree, pillar, landmark, ruin or doorway
4. No other text: only the zone name on its label plate. No title, no taglines, no captions, no swatch labels.

Style: hand-inked storybook game art (see FINISH below): thick wobbly brush-pen outlines in warm dark plum-grey (#373335) never black, mottled gouache washes with paper grain, chunky slightly crooked blocks, a few hand-drawn dash marks, one soft sun from the upper left (tops brightest, left sides 75%, right sides 55%), plain cream paper background (#eae8d6). Few, big, readable shapes: islands built from fewer, chunkier blocks rather than many small ones. Every zone uses the same block size and the same step height, but its OWN cliff material: root-tangled earth, cut masonry, ice shelf, layered sandstone, glassy rock and so on. Never the same grey stone blocks under every zone.

Colour: each zone has one main colour family plus ONE accent (example: volcanic = ash greys plus ember orange; crystal = slate plus violet). Accents are small: lava channels, crystals, lanterns. No saturated red or blue anywhere else.

Images: image 1 = style picture (the forest hike illustration), match its line and paint. Image 2 (optional) = an earlier sheet, match its layout only.

Avoid: perspective, 3d render, photo, vector art, clean digital cel shading, mobile game asset look, glossy highlights, gradients on ground, black outlines, characters, people, animals, skulls on every tile, busy clutter, watermark.

FINISH (hand-made, not polished)
Match the line and paint of the style picture if one is given (the forest hike illustration): it wins over everything else about surface.
Line: thick, wobbly brush-pen ink with uneven weight, slightly broken in places, warm dark plum-grey (#373335), never black, never clean vector strokes.
Paint: flat gouache or watercolour washes with mottled, blotchy texture and visible paper grain; colour sometimes slips a little past the line; no smooth gradients, no glossy highlights, no airbrush.
Marks: short dashes and ticks for grass, cracks and stone joints, drawn by hand, a few per surface, not a pattern.
Shapes: chunky and slightly crooked, like hand-cut wooden blocks: edges not perfectly straight but tops still flat and walkable, big simple masses, few small details.
Colour: few colours per zone (about 6), soft and earthy, a bit faded like a printed storybook.
Avoid: vector art, clean digital cel shading, mobile game asset store look, perfect geometry, uniform line weight, glossy, shiny, bevels, gradients, high saturation, tiny detail everywhere.
