02 · the rules

Shape, value and texture rules

These are what the code obeys. They are proposals until you mark them yours.

  • R1Big flat fields of color

    A scene is 3 to 5 flat planes. Sky and ground carry most of the color; detail is rare and small.

    snow village, figure on hill
  • R2Gradients only in skies

    Land and pieces are flat fills. A sky may fade from top to horizon. A plane may get one soft light band, never shading.

    mesa, cats on log
  • R3Soft, stacked, round forms

    Mountains are gumdrops stacked in rows, trees are scallops or lollipops. Corners are rounded, nothing is a sharp spike at home.

    cats on log, autumn wood
  • R4Marks instead of rendering

    Texture comes from stamped marks on a plane: dots, fans, ticks, little arcs. They follow the plane's color, a step lighter or darker.

    cats on log, figure on hill
  • R5No black, ever

    The darkest color in a region is a deep tint of its own hue: plum at home, navy in snow. Darks read like two inks printed over each other.

    riso-windowseat quality bar
  • R6Grain pinned to the world

    One paper grain and mottle over everything, seeded and fixed, so it never swims or flickers when the camera moves.

    riso-windowseat, autumn wood
  • R7Tiny pieces, huge world

    Pieces are small against the land and keep their own warm colors in every region. The world shifts, the pieces don't, so you always find your party.

    cats on log, figure on hill
  • R8One light source of accent

    Each region has one accent ink used for one thing: a lit window, a moon, the Lodestone's glow. Never sprinkled.

    snow village
  • R9Folk borders frame the picture

    The picture sits inside a decorated band built from repeating stamps (stars, trees, steps, checks). The band holds the UI.

    church, goat quilt
  • R10Strangeness inverts the values

    At home the sky is light and the land is dark. Past the Wall the land brightens and the sky darkens, until the deepest lands glow against a dark sky.

    my proposal for Earth to Alien
03 · region palettes

Nine inks per region, blended by distance

Each region is one row of nine roles. The game blends between rows as the party travels, so there is never a hard cut. The piece colors at the bottom never change.

04 · sample scenes

The same generator at five distances

Each scene is one call of the same code with a different strangeness. Mountains turn from gumdrops to mesas to floating rocks; trees turn from pines to lollipops to stalks, one tree at a time.

05 · the bottom frame

Three ways to tuck the UI into a border

Each mockup is the trail screen at the game's 16:9 size. All three move the party, items and Lodestone out of the side panels into one band along the bottom, so the picture gets the whole top of the screen. Labels are placeholders.

AEmbroidered band

From the church print. A thin golden border wraps the picture and thickens into a stitched band at the bottom. The Lodestone is a medallion that breaks the band's top edge and sits in the picture.

Reads as a storybook page. The gold band stays the same in every region, which anchors the strange lands but may feel heavy at home.

BQuilt row

From the goat quilt. The band is a row of quilt squares. Some are only stamps, others hold a party member, the Lodestone or items. Square colors come from the current region, so the frame drifts strange with the world.

Very modular and easy for code to extend, since one square is one thing. Busy if every square carries a stamp; it needs empty squares to breathe.

CForeground sill

From the cats on the log. The frame is the nearest layer of the landscape itself: dark trees at the sides, a ridge along the bottom. Pieces stand on it as real board pieces, items rest on it as tokens.

Most immersive and most screenshot-worthy. Hardest to keep readable when the inventory is full, and it must be redrawn per region.

06 · how we work

You direct, the code draws, this page is the table

This thread is the home for visual ideas, and this page is what we look at together. I learned the loop from riso-windowseat: write the rule first, prove the hardest picture first, keep every picture reproducible from a seed, and write down what is still weak.

  1. You react

    Reply in the thread or leave a comment on this page: "B, but with C's trees", "home is too purple", a new reference image.

  2. I change a rule

    Each change is a rule or a palette edit, not a one-off tweak, so it holds across every scene the game draws.

  3. I republish this page

    Same link, new draft number. Old drafts stay in the page history if we want to go back.

  4. You mark it yours

    When you like a rule, I record it in the style bible and decisions file as yours. Only then does it go into the game.

  5. I port it to Godot

    The same rules become shaders and draw scripts in the game, checked against this page with screenshots.

What the code builds

  • Landscape generator: layered planes driven by seed and strangeness
  • Region palettes as data, blended by distance
  • Paper grain and mottle as one fixed shader
  • Mark stamper that scatters dots, fans and ticks on planes
  • Pieces built from parts (base, body, head, hat, pack) for random members
  • The bottom frame as stamps on a 9-slice band

Where your hand matters most

  • Picking and killing rules on this page
  • Faces and hats for the pieces, if you want them hand drawn
  • A small set of stamps (star, tree, moon, motif) the code repeats
  • The hero moments: the Wall, first sight of the strange lands
  • All words. Labels here are placeholders

Questions for you
