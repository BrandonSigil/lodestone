# Fishing: three ways to play it [AI]

Brandon picked fishing as the first minigame (card master-e-minigame, 2026-10-31) and asked for three ideas: how it plays, how the party and the player can make it easier, and what unique rewards it can give. All three are [AI] proposals until he picks one on the desk card `design-fishing-three`.

What every version shares (already decided or built):
- A fishing spot is any dock or shore thing with the `fish` effect; FISH needs a rod in the pack (Brandon, 2026-10-30). A spot rests after one try until the next trip out of the gate.
- The catch goes in the pack as an ingredient. Pitch E's kitchen chain turns ingredients into meals that heal, lift morale and give a fight buff.
- Under a minute per try, playable one-handed on a phone.
- Catch quality has three grades: small, good and prize. A better grade makes a better meal.
- The first catch of each kind is recorded on the Lodestone's knowledge page (pitch C, approved).

## 1. The tense line (timing and tension)
- How it plays: cast, wait for the bite, then hold to reel. A bar shows a moving fish zone and your marker. Keep the marker inside the zone while a tension gauge fills. Too long outside and the fish slips; reel too hard and the line snaps.
- Making it easier: a better rod (smithy step) widens your marker. Bait chosen before the cast decides which fish come. A companion with a fishing knack slows the fish a little.
- Unique rewards: big fish fight harder and are worth more. A spot's prize fish only bites with the right bait.
- Cost to build: medium. It feels like Stardew, and it is the most skill-based of the three.

## 2. Read the water (choose, then strike)
- How it plays: the spot opens as a small flat top-down pool with fish shadows drifting. Their size and shape hint at what they are. You pick where to cast, the bobber lands, and when it dips you tap once in a short window. That's the whole game: a choice and a strike.
- Making it easier: the moon kit shows shadows clearly at night. Doggo can sniff out the prize shadow, or bark and scatter the pool. Bait draws one kind of shadow toward the bobber. A better rod lengthens the strike window.
- Unique rewards: each spot has a set of shadows that differ by region, time of day and weather. Past the Wall the shadows are unknown shapes, which ties into eating unknown food blind (pitch H).
- Cost to build: low to medium. It looks great in our flat style, it's easy on a phone, and every cast is a small decision.

## 3. Push your luck (the dice reel)
- How it plays: a board-game take that uses the d20 system we already have. Each cast draws a hidden fish token from the spot's pool. Reeling is a few steps, each a roll. After each step you can bank the catch at its current grade or push for a better one. A bad roll snaps the line, and you lose the catch.
- Making it easier: party members add dice or rerolls (the hunter +1, the chanter one reroll). Bait reshapes the pool. A better rod gives one free push.
- Unique rewards: pushing through to the last step catches the spot's prize. Some tokens are oddities that aren't food, like a key or a sealed bottle. Those are slots for Brandon's text, never written by us.
- Cost to build: lowest. It reads like the rest of the game's checks and fits the board-game pieces.

## Our pick
2, Read the water. It's the most beautiful (a screenshot moment by the dock), the friendliest on a phone, and each cast is a choice rather than a reflex. The party's kits change what you can see, which ties into pitch D. 3 is the cheapest fallback if we want it in sooner.
