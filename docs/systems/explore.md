# Exploration maps (graybox)

Code: `game/scripts/explore/`. Scene: `scenes/explore.tscn` (F6 on it, or **F10 from any screen**). Rules math is in `explore_rules.gd` (pure, tested in `tests/test_explore.gd`). Decisions are under "Exploration maps" in `docs/decisions.md`.

## How it plays
- The party leader is the one piece on the map; the rest of the party travels with them unseen. Click ground to walk there (the path previews on hover; paths take diagonals that don't cut a corner, and the leader glides at an even 5 cells a second with a hop only where the ground changes height). WASD walks freely, not cell by cell: the leader slides along walls and eases round corners (`ExploreRules.slide`, body half-width 0.26 cells), and each new cell it crosses into checks sight and enemies like a step on a path. Arrows or right drag move the camera, wheel zooms (1.0 to 1.8, starting at 1.35: [USER] 2026-10-29, close in so what's on screen is what the party gets), Space snaps back to the leader. The view never turns: each map has one approved angle ([USER] 2026-10-28).
- **Staging.** Outdoor maps use the painted look's staging, as in the map maker and fights: the soft terrain pass, the set's skyline wallpaper fixed behind the view and the solid foreground band (`CombatBoard.wallpaper`). Interiors (look `plain`) keep their dark floor.
- **Fog of war is off for now** ([USER] 2026-10-28): every map draws whole and every enemy group stands in view. Spotting still works by line of sight within 7 cells (you see them first, or they see you). `ExploreRules.FOG_OF_WAR = true` brings back each map's own `fog` setting, as described next.
- **Locked rooms stay fogged** ([USER] 2026-10-29). Ground the party can only reach through a shut door or rubble (`ExploreRules.locked_cells`), and the walls that face only that ground, is not drawn, and enemies and things in it stay hidden, until the way opens. Worked out on arrival and after each "open" effect, from every cell the party could walk to so far (`ExploreRules.reach`). A door shows while it touches ground the party can reach; a door deeper in (off a locked corridor) hides with its corridor. This works with fog of war off or on.
- **Walls maps** ([USER] 2026-10-29). A map with `walls: true` (the crypt, the wolf den, every house interior) draws its rock as plain grey walls: flat faces in the dungeon cliff's grey (`CombatBoard.WALL_SIDE`, a lighter `WALL_TOP`), square corners, no boulders. On arrival, after the doors shut, `ExploreRules.shape_walls` sorts the rock: a back wall (nothing walkable behind it) stands `WALL_TALL` (3 levels) over the floor beside it; a wall with floor in front and behind stands `WALL_HALF` (1 level); a front wall (floor behind, none in front but a doorway) becomes walkable floor like the floor behind it, and the board stands a stubby wall `RIM_THIN` (a quarter cell) deep and `RIM_HIGH` (1 level) tall on its outer edge. A run of front-edge floor that would join two areas a Jump 1 walker couldn't get between (round a shut door) stays a half wall. Rock touching no floor is void. A walls map can list `outdoors` zones (set names, as painted in the map maker; the crypt lists `forest`): their rock between two floors stays natural rock and their edge rock is void, so the forest skirt's tree line frames the area, except a back edge facing a pocket the world's edge never reaches (the space between the yard and the buildings), which is a tall wall. `shape_walls` returns the wall cells and the rim cells; the board styles exactly those. Wall kits ([USER] 2026-10-30, `docs/art/references/interior-walls-target.webp`): a walls map with `wall_kit` (an art set: `village_in` for houses, `dungeon` for the crypt and the den) has thin back walls, not blocks. `shape_walls(..., kit)` keeps back-wall cells at the floor's height and returns `backs` (cell -> the sides facing the room, two at a corner); the board stands a thin wall `PIECE_HIGH` (6) levels tall on each (`CombatBoard._wall_piece`) and wraps the kit's flat textures onto it like a cliff side (one square = 2 cells by 4 levels): the bottom square is `wall_plain` (now and then `wall_worn` / `wall_cracked`), or a `wall_window` / `wall_door` placed as a prop, which spans its cell and the next along the wall; the upper half of `wall_top` (`wall_cap` in dungeons) above it, so the beam or capping runs along the wall's top edge; the bottom fifth of `wall_foot` along the foot if the kit has one; and at an inside corner only a post `POST_SIZE` square wearing `wall_timber` (`wall_corner`), where the two walls meet. Floors use the set's own `top_stone`, `top_stone_b` and `side_masonry`. Until the art lands, a graybox plane with dark windows and doorways. House interiors have walls only along the back of the room (`build_village.py` `room()`; the front edges are open floor, shown by the floor's edge tile) with `window()` and `door()` pieces; the dungeons' front walls stay thin stubs. A map can also set `wall_tall` for block back walls. The map maker still draws these maps the old way.
- **Fog of war (when on).** The leader sees 7 cells, blocked by the same line-of-sight rule as combat (walls, pillars, cliffs, trees). Never-seen cells are not drawn. Seen cells stay on the map, dimmed, once out of sight. The party can only path through cells it has seen. A map with `fog: false` (the village) shows every cell from the start.
- **Enemies.** Groups stand on the map and are drawn only while in sight. Hover one to see the cells it watches (red).
  - You see them first: the walk stops and a box asks FIGHT, SNEAK HIT (sneak check: win = the group sleeps through its first phase and the party may deploy a step further out; lose = ambush), TALK or CALM (persuade or tame: win = they let you pass), BACK OFF.
  - They see you first (you walk into their sight): ambush, the enemy moves first.
  - Other groups within 3 cells of the fight join it.
- **Fights happen in place.** The game goes turn based on the same map. First you deploy: the leader stays put; each other member gets a blue ring of cells around the leader (ring = move - 3 steps, at least 1; +1 on a sneak attack; 1 when ambushed). It starts auto-placed. Pick a member on the right, click a blue cell, ENTER or START FIGHT. Then it is the normal battle (`Battle` borrows the explore board). Win: loot from the group, downed members stand back up with 1 HP, the others fall back in with the leader. Lose: the Lodestone recalls the party.
- **Things.** Doors, rubble, chests, a hum stone, the way out. Within 3 cells a bobbing "!" icon appears; click the thing or press F to walk up and use it. A box shows the options this party has (an option can need a skill, class or character) with who rolls and the chance. Results apply to the run (food, gold, HP) and the map (door opens, stone reveals the whole map, noise draws the nearest group).

## Data
- `data/explore/<name>.json`: `grid` (same letters and height digits as sandbox maps, plus optional `props`), `start` (leader cell first), `groups` (`id`, `event`, `sight`, `loot`, `units` with class id and cell), `things` (`id`, `kind` door / rubble / chest / shrine / exit / enter / rack / hidden, `event`, `cell`, `floor` = terrain letter a door or rubble becomes when opened, `to` and `at` = the map and cell an `enter` thing leads to, `once` = flags like `a|b`: when any is set the thing is gone for good), `art_set`, `strangeness`, `view`, `turn` (false locks the camera), `fog` (false shows every cell). Doors and rubble are forced to ruin terrain while shut.
- New thing kinds for the E2E loop: `statue`, `cache`, `window`, `dog`, `climb` (drawn by `ExploreOverlay`). A thing's `when` flag hides it until the flag is set. A group's `boss` pays boss XP and `end` ends the E2E test when it is beaten.
- Option needs also take `item:<id>` (the pack holds one), `flag:<id>` and `noflag:<id>`.
- Thing kinds: `enter` with no event walks straight through to `to` (a doorway); with an event it opens the box first (`door_stuck`), and the `travel` effect goes through. `hidden` has no marker and can't be hovered or used until the leader stands next to it. Walking out of a map remembers what was seen and used there, and which enemy groups were beaten or calmed, until the party leaves through the exit (Brandon's E2E notes, 2026-10-28). Stepping into a map through a door next to a group never ambushes: the party gets the first look and the FIGHT / SNEAK HIT / CALM / BACK OFF box. Nor does a group pounce right after a choice (BACK OFF included) before the party moves.
- Clicks and the hover land on floor: the explore board sets `CombatBoard.pick_floor`, so walls, rock and anything nobody stands on never take them, and the floor you see behind a front wall or curb wins (Brandon's bug, 2026-10-30). A thing's cell still does (`pick_also`: a shut door, the rack). That covers walking, move and attack targets in fights and the range hover. The map maker keeps picking walls.
- Fishing ([USER] 2026-10-30): any thing whose event has an option with the `fish` map effect fishes there, with the world map's FISH roll; the spot rests until the next trip out of town (`Game.use_for_trip`). It needs a rod in the pack (`rod` in `data/items.csv`, gear, 3 x 1), at a dock (the row's FISH needs `item:rod`, and a NO ROD line needs `noitem:rod`) and on the world map (`TravelRules.can_try`, the FISH button greys out) alike. The first rod is a one-time find: a thing of kind `find` whose event is its effects (`rod:+1`), taken for good with its `got_<map>_<id>` flag like the other lasting rewards.
- Doorways (`enter`) and ways out (`exit`) don't block: the party walks over them, and F standing on one uses it (`ExploreRules.WALK_ON_KINDS`; Brandon's bug 2026-10-30, a door's "!" on the lane was an invisible wall). Every other thing blocks its cell; a shut `door` or `rubble` until it opens. F uses the thing the overlay marks with its F (`ExploreRules.thing_to_use`): the nearest shown thing within `HINT_RANGE` on a lit cell, a tie going to the one the leader faces. `tests/test_loop.gd` walks every map: every door and thing can be reached and used, and F answers every hint.
- Placed props (`grid.props`, `CombatGrid.add_prop`): `sprite` (file stem in the cell's tile set), `cell` (back corner), `foot` (cells along x and y), `tall` (draw height in tile widths; 0 = a building that spans its footprint with its bottom on the front corner), `sight` (levels it blocks sight), `walk` (rugs). Every footprint cell is blocked unless `walk`. They draw with the footprint cell nearest the camera. When one hides a piece, a soft window opens in it round the piece (`CombatBoard._draw_windowed`: `WINDOW_R` radius, fully open over the inner `WINDOW_CLEAR` share, `WINDOW_MIN` alpha there) and the rest stays solid; what the window clears still shows as a faint flat wash in the prop's shape (`WINDOW_TINT`); a party piece only props hide gets just its foot ring (`WINDOW_RING_ONLY`), and the tinted x-ray copy stays for pieces a cliff hides. Trees and arches open the same way. Only full-height things open it ([USER] 2026-10-30): buildings, arches, and props at least `WINDOW_TALL` (0.9 tile, a little over a piece) tall that the catalog marks `see` (`data/catalog.csv`: trees, pillars, walls, a roofed well or stall, tall furniture) or doesn't list; a tile set's terrain props go by the same height (forest yes, rocks no). Rocks, bushes, fences, crates, carts, benches, tables, stools, barrels, signs, lamps and flower boxes stay solid (`CombatBoard.see_through`). Buildings have their door on the left front wall (facing +y), so the door cell is the one in front of it; a piece standing right in front of a building's wall draws after the building (`CombatBoard._unit_depth`), so it never looks behind the house it stands at. A prop's `open` (footprint offsets `"x,y|x,y"`, `CombatGrid.open_offsets`) leaves those cells free: the smithy's ^ shape leaves its yard cell open, and its door is there, inside the footprint (`build_village.py`: `house(..., yard=(2, 2))`). The map maker keeps `open` when it mirrors a prop; it has no field for it yet.
- A tile set may carry `data/battle_art_<set>.csv` (same columns as `battle_art.csv`) to change which files a terrain uses there: in `village_in` rock is a plaster wall with no boulders.
- `data/explore_events.csv`: one row per event, up to 4 options. Each option: `key` (text), `need` ("" / `skill:<check>` / `class:<id>` / `char:<id>`), `check` (a trail check id or `none`), `dc`, `win`, `lose`. Effects mix run effects (`food:+3`, `hp_one:-2`, as in `events.csv`) with map effects: `open`, `remove`, `reveal`, `noise`, `exit`, `fight`, `sneak_attack`, `ambushed`, `pacify`.
- Text keys `EX_*`, `OPT_*`, `UI_EX_*` in `text/placeholder.csv` (caveman placeholders).
- The first map `crypt` (34 x 30) is generated by `tools/explore/build_crypt.py`: entry yard, a corridor with a locked door, a sealed side crypt with a chest, a sunken water channel with a raised bridge and waterfall, a cave room behind rubble with wolves, a central plaza of pillars with bandits, a graveyard with a watch tower, and a throne terrace up a grand stair with the way out.
- `village_v2` (56 x 56, `tools/explore/build_village_v2.py`) is a second village build, its own level on the launch menu only ([USER] 2026-10-30, `docs/art/references/village-v2-layout.webp`). Pass 2 after his review: every district is a noisy blob at its own height (lake 0 and shore 1, meadow 2, south town and east homes 3, tavern 4, farm 5, square plateau and the ruins' foot 6, ruins and hall 8), so cliffs several levels tall stand between them and no outline is a rectangle. The stream runs down a gorge whose floor drops 6, 4, 2, 0, falling at each step; a stone bridge and a footbridge cross it (decks). `road()` lays winding courses and cuts a stair flight wherever a road meets a cliff, one level a step, with a top step so it reads up its course. Then `dress_explore.gd` applies V9 (tidy) and V10 (pillars). Its doors and gate stay shut (`door_stuck`); `companion_spots` mark where each district's companions will stand. The shared builder parts (Map, ribbon, poly, flight, `check_village`, deck-aware, keeping out of water and woods like the game) are in `tools/explore/village_kit.py`.
- `city_proto` (44 x 44, `tools/explore/build_city_proto.py`) is a layout test for a dense town: an upper terrace four levels over the lower town, a three-wide grand stair, lanes two cells wide at least, gray box buildings with fall houses along the street fronts, walkable roofs a storey up, and houses on real bridges you walk under (decks, `docs/systems/decks.md`). No fights or things yet.

## Balance tools
- `godot --headless --path game -s res://tools/explore_balance.gd -- [fights level weapon]`: AI-vs-AI win rates for the hero and the dog against every group on the E2E maps and the road-fight pairs. Quick, but it guesses where the party stands.
- `godot --headless --path game -s res://tools/fight_probe.gd -- <map> <group> [tries]`: the real thing. Starts the map, walks the leader in from the start with the real walk, and lets the fight play on auto. This is what showed the pass wolves winning 12 of 12 from the meadow below their shelf.

Group data (in each map's JSON) also takes `goals`, `exit`, `talk` and `elite`, and a unit entry `leader: true` (fight goals and TALK: `docs/systems/combat.md`; elites: `docs/systems/overworld.md`). `ExploreMap` keeps them (it dropped them before 2026-10-30).

Lasting rewards ([USER] omr-10; one-more-run step 6): three thing kinds, taken for good (the flag `got_<map>_<thing id>`). `boost` (event `hp:2`, `damage:1`, `armor:1`, `magic:1` or `crit:1`): a box asks which party member takes it, and it adds to that member for good (`found` in their level state). `key` (event = the flag it sets): a door, cache or shortcut on its map opens on that flag through its own event's need (`flag:<key>`). `rare` (event = effects, like `rare:1;metal:2`): rare materials into the pack for the village's restore steps. Placeholder drawings: a gem, a key, a sparkling lump. The map builder places them.
