Lodestone · pipeline and art direction

Build it cheap, make it look hand-made

A plan for a near-free Godot pipeline around your Claude plan, and an art system that keeps every mark yours. The scene above is a working example of the layered parallax and the Earth-to-Alien dial described below. Move your pointer over it, then drag the slider.

Part one

The agent studio

The Reddit dev spent about $800 over 11 weeks, and $600 of it was Claude Max. Everything else was paid generators you will not need, because you are the artist. Your real cost is your Claude plan plus maybe $40 of optional tools.

What to drop from the pasted setup

Unreal Engine 5Built for 3D. Godot's 2D renderer is lighter, free, and its text-based scene files (.tscn) are easy for Claude to read and edit.
Blender + AI 3D modelsNot needed for a flat, hand-drawn look. If you ever want a reference pose, a free mannequin in Blender is enough.
GPT image / Higgsfield artThis is the slop risk. Use image tools only for private mood boards you never ship, or skip them.
"extra" and "ultra code" effortThose names don't match Claude Code. Its effort levels are low, medium, high, xhigh and max. Pick per session, as in the table below.
Orca / Codex side-agentsNot needed. Claude Code subagents and project threads already split work in parallel.

What goes in CLAUDE.md

- Never write story, dialogue, names or flavor text.
  All player-facing text lives in text/placeholder.csv as caveman speak.
- Never generate or edit art files. Art comes only from art/source.
- Read docs/decisions.md before any design change. [USER] wins.
- Game data lives in data/*.tres, not hardcoded in scripts.
- After a change: run tools/check.sh (headless import + tests) and fix errors.

Godot tooling that lets Claude see its own work

  • godot-mcp by Coding-Solo (free, MIT). It launches the editor, runs the project, reads console errors and creates scenes and nodes. The Summer Engine roundup rates it the best free option for stock Godot. GDAI MCP ($19 one-time) adds screenshots and simulated input if you want them later.
  • Headless CLI. godot --headless --path game --import and a test run catch broken scripts without opening the editor. Put both in tools/check.sh so every session runs it.
  • Tests. GUT or gdUnit4 for the rules code: dice math, food math, pathing. Your feel tests stay manual.
  • Screenshot harness. A tiny debug scene that loads a level, waits two frames and saves get_viewport().get_texture().get_image() to PNG. Claude can then look at its shader work instead of guessing.
  • Git + GitHub (free). Commit every working state. Use Git LFS for big art files.

Habits that stretch your plan

  • Graybox first. Colored squares on a grid until combat is fun. Art goes on top of a game you already like playing.
  • One task per message to a system session, with the doc it should read. Vague asks burn the most usage.
  • Use medium effort for data entry and UI, and save xhigh for combat rules and shaders.
  • When a procedure repeats (adding a class, importing an art batch), turn it into a skill so it runs the same way every time.
  • Watch out for the Reddit dev's trap. His game got called a Dredge clone because one recognizable mechanic was copied whole. Your Oregon Trail and tactics mix is distinct. Keep each borrowed system bent toward your own rules.
Part two

Beautiful, cohesive, and yours

AI slop looks like slop because nothing holds it together: no fixed palette, no fixed line, no fixed shape rules. The Banner Saga looks expensive for the opposite reason. Arnie Jorgensen picked one master influence, Eyvind Earle's Sleeping Beauty backgrounds, tested it by cutting an Earle painting into parallax layers, and then drew everything with simple line art and flat fills. You can work the same way.

Style bible, one page, before any sprite

  • Line. One brush, one weight per scale (pieces 3px, props 2px, far scenery none). Line color is a dark of the fill, never black.
  • Fill. Flat color plus one shade step and one optional texture overlay. No soft airbrush gradients on characters.
  • Palette. A master palette of about 24 swatches arranged as ramps. Each region uses 5 to 7 of them plus one accent. Shaders handle time of day, so you never repaint.
  • Shape language. Earth: tall verticals and stacked horizontals, Earle's pines and banded hills. Past the Wall: curves, bulbs, things floating, wrong scale.
  • Texture. One paper grain overlay over the whole screen, applied by a shader. It ties every asset together for free.
  • Value plan. Far layers are light and low contrast. The play layer has the darkest darks. Players read depth before they read color.
  • Do and don't sheet. Five of your drawings that are on-model, five that aren't. Every session gets this file as a reference.

Board-game pieces as paper dolls

Each character is a stack of small drawings snapped to the same anchor points. You draw each part once in grayscale. A palette-swap shader maps the grays to any color ramp. Two pawn bodies, eight hats, six held items, three face sets and four capes give you thousands of distinct pieces. The tray below rolls a random party from that kind of kit.

6 pieces

Parts list per piece

  • Base: pawn or meeple silhouette with a round foot disc
  • Head: circle, with a face layer of dot eyes plus one mark
  • Top: hood, hat, helm, leaf crown, or bush for Bushberry
  • Hand: staff, bow, blade, book, lantern
  • Back: cape, quiver, book bag (the Wizard's slot cost, shown)
  • Quirk: one tiny prop that says who they are, like a feather or a patch

Animation without frames

  • Move is a hop, like lifting a piece off the board: squash, rise, land, wobble. One Tween, every character.
  • Idle is a slow breathing scale of 2%, plus a blink swap every few seconds.
  • Hit is a tilt and a shake. Spooked (Stardrop) is a fast jitter and wider eyes.
  • Personality comes from timing, not detail. A frail piece hops lower, a big one lands harder.
  • Use hand-drawn frames only for hero moments like a spell or a death, the way Banner Saga saved full animation for key beats.

Screenshot moments: the layer stack

Every travel and camp scene is built from the same seven layers. Each one is cheap on its own. Together they produce the "stop and screenshot" frame.

Add a photo mode early: hide the frame UI, freeze time, let the player nudge the camera. Players who screenshot are your marketing.

Earth to Alien: one number

Give every region a Strangeness value from 0 to 1, and let every visual system read it. The slider in the header shows this working. Line weight, paper grain and the piece style never change, so the world stays recognizably one game while everything else drifts.

The framed border

  • Draw the border once as a 9-slice (NinePatchRect) so it scales to any screen.
  • Make the party strip from the same paper-doll pieces as the board. The piece you grow attached to is literally always on screen.
  • Show inventory as physical slots. The Wizard's books sit there taking up room, and Bushberry adds one ration icon each day.
  • Let the border react: frost creeps in when cold, vines grow past the Wall. One shader with a mask you paint.
  • Build all of it on a single Godot Theme resource, so fonts and colors change in one place.
Order of work

First six weeks

  1. Week 0: set up. Repo, CLAUDE.md, decisions.md, godot-mcp, check script. Write the one-page style bible and pick the master palette.
  2. Weeks 1 and 2: graybox combat. Iso grid with height, three test units, dice checks, one enemy type. No art.
  3. Week 3: graybox trail. Travel days, food and morale ticking, one caveman event with fight, talk and sneak.
  4. Week 4: one beautiful screen. A single camp scene with all seven layers and your art. This is the vertical slice of the look.
  5. Week 5: paper-doll rig. Your first parts kit, the palette-swap shader, and the hop and idle tweens on the combat board.
  6. Week 6: the frame. Border, party strip and inventory slots wired to real data, then a playtest loop from village to fight to camp and back.

Each step ends with something you can play or screenshot. If a step drags past its week, cut scope inside it instead of starting the next.
